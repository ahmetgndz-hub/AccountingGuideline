# IFRS 15 Revenuie from contracts with customers

## Revenue

The Companies have applied IFRS 15 "Revenue from contracts with customers", which has replaced IAS 18, by using cumulative effect method on the transition date, 1 January 2018. In accordance with this method, The Companies record the cumulative effect related to the transition of IFRS 15 in retained earnings on the first application date. Therefore, prior year financial statements are not restated and these financial statements are presented in accordance with IAS 18.

In accordance with this method of transition, the Companies calculated the impact of transition for the active contacts as of 1 January 2018 and the adoption did not effect recognition and measurement as disclosed below.

IFRS 15 specifies how and when revenue should be recognized as well as requires more detailed revenue disclosures. The standard provides a single, principle based five-step model to be applied to all contracts with customers and the Companies have applied the five-step model framework on revenue 

- Step 1: Identify the contract(s) with a customer
- Step 2: Identify the performance obligations in the contract
- Step 3: Determine the transaction price
- Step 4: Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations in the contract
- Step 5: Recognize revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation

Revenue comprises rental incomes and service charge incomes. The following is a description of the principal activities from which the Companies generate their revenue.

## Rental income

The Companies are the lessor in operating leases. Rental income (base rent, turnover rent and mall income received from tenants) arising from operating leases on investment property is accounted for on a straight-line basis over the lease terms and is included in revenue in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income due to its operating nature, except for contingent rental income which is recognized when it arises.

Turnover rents represent the rent that are linked to annual sales of tenants; either for the part that is exceeding or replacing the tenants' contractual base rents.

Mall income represent any income from renting space as such rents received from kiosks, temporary stands, ATMs, antennas and temporary storages.

Initial direct costs incurred in negotiating and arranging an operating lease are recognized as an expense over the lease term on the same basis as the lease income.

Discounts, which primarily comprised operational support and rent-free periods provided to tenants, are recognized as a reduction of rental revenue on a straight-line basis over the term of the lease.

Amounts received from tenants to terminate leases or to compensate for dilapidations are recognized in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income when the right to receive them arises.

The rental income recognized within the scope of IAS 17.

Service charge income

Service charge income arising from common area expenses (majorly arising from utilities, cleaning, security, maintenance expenses and management fees comprising the mall management activities) recharged to tenants is recognized in the period in which the compensation becomes receivable. The management evaluates this revenue stream as single performance obligation only and considered within the scope of IFRS 15.

The Companies allocate the transaction price to the performance obligation based on the stand-alone selling price.

Revenue is measured based on the consideration specified in a contract with a customer. Service charge income is recognized overtime since the customer simultaneously receives and consumes all of the benefits provided by the Companies performance as the Companies perform. The Companies recognize revenue on the basis of direct measurements of the value to the customer of the goods or services transferred to date relative to the remaining goods or services promised under the contract with output method.

The Companies act as principal for the service charges since the Companies are primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified service and the Companies have discretion in establishing prices for the specified services, therefore, obtains substantially all of the remaining benefits. Revenue is recognized on a gross basis on the financial statements.

## Key accounting estimates and judgements

Management performs significant accounting estimates in connection with determining the appropriate income recognition of contract elements. The Companies yearly budget figures are used in applying the revenue standard. While preparing the budget for the Companies, the management rely on the existing service contracts and apply its best estimate that are in line with the market practice for the other service charge expenses. Estimates and assumptions are reviewed on an on-going basis, and changes in estimates and assumptions are recognized in the period in which the changes occur and in future periods impacted by the changes.

The Companies receive advance payments from tenants with respect to service charges in accordance with the approved budget for the period. The invoices are issued to the tenants associated with the budgeted figures on a monthly basis as the Companies reconcile and settle the difference between the actual and the budgeted service charges by the end of each calendar year.
