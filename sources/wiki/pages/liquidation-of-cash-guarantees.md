# Liquidation of cash guarantees

**Process of cash guarantees**

At the start of the lease term, the owner receives rental deposits from the tenant. These are recorded in the tenant deposit account and parked at balance sheet account 25550 when received to the owner's bank account. We had to utilize tenant deposits/guarantees to cover the tenant's outstanding debt in some cases. When we decided to liquidate amounts to an A/R account, this used to be done via a "manual journal" and was not picked up by the cash flow statements.

 

**Example booking when deposit received (mostly before lease agreement starts)**

| *Document code * | *BANK* |
|---|---|
| 13311 Bank | DR 500 |
| 25550 Cash deposit | CR 500 |

* *

**Problem**

In some cases, the Multi fee structure is based on collections. As explained above, the utilization of cash guarantees didn't hit collections / rental bank account; therefore, it was not included in the direct cash flow statement. As a result, it might have been overlooked in a "manual" fee calculation. Direct cash flow statement increasingly used by countries to calculate fee income and providing data to 3rd party managers.

 

**Solution and bookkeeping instruction**

For proper follow-up cash transaction and not to lose any fee income, please make sure that your teams are following the below methodology (whether or not an asset has collection-related fee income or not) :

 

**Example booking when utilizing cash guarantee**

| *Document code * | **JV-CFCORR** |
|---|---|
| 25550 Cash deposit | DR 500 |
| 13111 A/R control account | CR 500 |

If it's a liquidation of a "cash-backed guarantee" (e.g., Bank Letter of Guarantee, etc.) and hits the asset's bank account when it's liquidated, you don't need to make the above booking as it already a cash transaction. These kinds of guarantees are not in our bank accounts yet.

 

P.S.: Please consider making an actual bank transfer from the tenant deposit bank account to the current account if loan agreements oblige you to keep tenant deposits in a separate bank account.
