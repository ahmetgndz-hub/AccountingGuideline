# Accounting Treatment of Leases under IFRS 16 — V 08-04-2021

*Memo attached to the old Wiki "Accounting Manual" page (PDF export of Word document "20210408 IFRS 16 Accounting for Leases Accounting Manual_v2"). No author named in the document body.*

## 1. Introduction, purpose and content

IFRS 16 "Leases" introduces a single, on-balance sheet lease accounting model for lessees. This Memo outlines the recognition and measurement principles for the lessee in a lease arrangement. IFRS means to show the 'right of use' of the asset, and the liability to pay for using that asset, both explicitly on the balance sheet.

For Multi the applicable Leases can be categorized as follows:

- Office Rent
- Car leases
- Office equipment leases (copiers / printers)

## 2. Recognition and measurement of Leases

1. The transaction price is the amount to which an entity expects to be entitled in exchange for the transfer of goods and services.

Upon lease commencement a lessee recognizes a right-of-use asset representing its right to use the underlying asset and a lease liability representing its obligation to make lease payments.

**Lease asset**

Under the cost model a right-of-use asset is measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment. The right-of-use asset is initially measured at the amount of the lease liability plus any initial direct costs incurred by the lessee. Adjustments may also be required for lease incentives, payments at or prior to commencement and restoration obligations or similar.

**Lease liability**

The lease liability is initially measured at the NPV (net present value) of the lease payments payable over the lease term, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease if that can be readily determined. If that rate cannot be readily determined, the lessee shall use their incremental borrowing rate.

## 3. Detailed guidance on measurement

After lease commencement, a lessee shall measure the right-of-use asset using a cost model, unless:

- the right-of-use asset is an investment property which is valued at fair value under IAS 40; or
- the right-of-use asset relates to a class of PPE to which the lessee applies IAS 16's revaluation model, in which case all right-of-use assets relating to that class of PP&E can be revalued.

Variable lease payments that depend on an index or a rate are included in the initial measurement of the lease liability and are initially measured using the index or rate as at the commencement date. Amounts expected to be payable by the lessee under residual value guarantees are also included.

Variable lease payments that are not included in the measurement of the lease liability are recognised in profit or loss in the period in which the event or condition that triggers payment occurs.

The lease liability is subsequently remeasured to reflect changes in:

1. the lease term (using a revised discount rate);
2. the assessment of a purchase option (using a revised discount rate);
3. the amounts expected to be paid under residual value guarantees (using an unchanged discount rate);
4. future lease payments resulting from a change in an index or a rate used to determine those payments (using an unchanged discount rate).

Lease modifications may also prompt remeasurement of the lease liability unless they are to be treated as separate leases.

Lease Accounting follows the below steps:

### 1. Identifying a lease

At inception of a contract, an entity is required to assess whether the contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or contains, a lease if the contract conveys the right to control the use if an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for a consideration.

### 2. Separating components of a contract

For a contract that contains a lease component and additional lease and non-lease components, such as the lease of an asset and the provision of a maintenance service, lessees shall allocate the consideration payable on the basis of the relative stand-alone prices, which shall be estimated if observable prices are not readily available.

As a practical expedient, a lessee may elect, by class of underlying asset, not to separate non-lease components from lease components and instead account for all components as a lease.

### 3. Lease term

The lease term is defined as "the non-cancellable period for which a lessee has the right to use an underlying asset, plus:

a) periods covered by an extension option if exercise of that option by the lessee is reasonably certain; and
b) periods covered by a termination option if the lessee is reasonably certain not to exercise that option"

As part of its assessment of the lease term and the length of the non-cancellable period of a lease, an entity should consider the definition of a contract ("an agreement between two or more parties that creates enforceable rights and obligations") and determine the period for which the contract is enforceable.

A lease is no longer enforceable when the lessee and the lessor each has the right to terminate the lease without permission from the other party with no more than an insignificant penalty.

### 4. Discount rates

**Interest rate implicit in the lease**

The interest rate that yields a present value of (a) the lease payments and (b) the unguaranteed residual value equal to the sum of (i) the fair value of the underlying asset and (ii) any initial direct costs of the lessor.

**Lessee's incremental borrowing rate**

The rate of interest that a lessee would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with a similar security, the funds necessary to obtain an asset of a similar value to the right-of-use asset in a similar economic environment.

**Recognition exemptions**

Instead of applying the recognition requirements of IFRS 16, a lessee may elect to account for lease payments as an expense on a straight-line basis over the lease term or another systematic basis for the following two types of leases:

1. leases with a lease term of 12 months or less and containing no purchase options – this election is made by class of underlying asset; and
2. leases where the underlying asset has a low value when new (such as personal computers or small items of office furniture) – this election can be made on a lease-by-lease basis.

## 4. Impact Analysis

**Accounting Approach**

- The identification of the contracts and the assets are considered to be straight-forward based on a standard lease contract.
- Multi has opted not to perform a detailed review of non-lease components (e.g. service delivery) included in the leases.
- The term applied is the term of the lease. Typically, these contracts do not include options for extension or options to purchase the asset after the contract. Early termination is usually subject to a penalty and is likely to occur in case an employee leaves the Multi group. No estimation for a chance of leave are implemented.
- The right-of-use asset is based on the total contract value and depreciated over the lifetime of the contract. The lease obligation will be equal to the remaining contract value.
- For the recognition and measurement we refer to Chapter 2

**Disclosure**

- Inclusion of movements in the capitalized Lease asset and Lease liability, as part of the movement schedule of PP&E
- Under the Lease Arrangements note to the Financial Statements, we disclose the following:
  - Carrying value opening balance
  - Carrying value closing balance
  - Depreciation charge for the year (lease asset)
  - Lease payments (lease liability)
  - Interest charge (lease liability)
