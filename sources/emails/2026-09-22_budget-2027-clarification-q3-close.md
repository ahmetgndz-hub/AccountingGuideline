They apply to all countries and to all entities in scope, and replace any guidance issued in previous budget cycles. The budget cycle covers two parts. **Part A — Service Companies**: the entities employing staff and managing assets and shopping centres on behalf of Property Owners. **Part B — Owned Assets**: the assets owned, or partly owned, by Multi in Ukraine, Italy and the Netherlands, for which a full shopping centre budget is required. **Part C** applies to all entities in scope. **Three points that apply to every submission** Use the attached RMA Presentation template only. PART A — SERVICE COMPANIES
**2.  Fee Income Guidelines and Underlying Assumptions2.1  Basis of preparation**

Budgeted fee income for the Service Companies must be derived directly from the approved budget of the underlying real estate portfolio actively managed by Multi. Budgeting must remain strictly faithful to the management agreements signed with the asset owners.

Each fee must be budgeted in line with its contractual definition, fee rate, calculation base, cap or floor and billing frequency as set out in the agreement. Simplified, blended or rule-of-thumb assumptions are not acceptable.

Where the agreement links a fee to a driver of the underlying asset budget — projected Net Operating Income, Rental Income, Service Charges, Collections, capex or project spend, leasing transactions, and similar — the figures used must be identical to those in the approved asset budget, so that the fee income of the Service Company and the corresponding fee expense of the asset fully reconcile.

**2.2  Level of detail and upload level**

All fees must be budgeted at full contractual detail, at the level of the individual fee type — that is, at the el4 + el5 combination level.

**Mandatory upload level — fee income**

el1 + el2 (asset) + el4 + el5

Submissions that do not carry all four dimensions, or that are uploaded at an aggregated level, will be rejected.

PART B — OWNED ASSETS (UKRAINE, ITALY, THE NETHERLANDS)

For the Multi-owned assets, a full shopping centre budget is required, prepared with the same rigour and level of detail as the budgets prepared for third-party Property Owners. The following sections set out the minimum content.
**5.  Rental Income and Leasing Assumptions5.1  Rental income**
–
Budget rental income at tenant and unit level, based on the contracted rent roll and in accordance with the signed lease agreements.
–
Re-assess the annual ERV per unit; ideally the total should increase with CPI compared to last year.
–
Apply indexation in line with the indexation clauses of the individual leases, using the HQ CPI assumptions.
–
Budget turnover rent separately, based on projected tenant sales and the contractual provisions for turnover rent.

**5.2  Leasing assumptions**
–
Reflect lease expiries and break options in 2027, with explicit renewal and re-letting assumptions: renewal probability, ERV and expected downtime.
–
Budget incentives — rent-free periods, temporary discounts and fit-out contributions — separately, and state the underlying assumptions.
–
Outline the vacancy assumptions (frictional vacancy, active vacancy management and structural vacancy), consistent with the definitions used in Part A.
**6.  Service Charge and Other Income6.1  Service charge budget**
–
Prepare a detailed service charge budget per asset, showing recoverable income against recoverable costs.
–
Quantify the owner’s share separately: void and vacancy costs, contractual caps, and any leakage — the non-recoverable portion of service charge costs — stating the assumed recovery rate per asset.

**6.2  Other income**

Budget other income lines separately:
–
Parking income
–
Specialty leasing and mall income (kiosks, promotions, temporary lettings)
–
Media and advertising income
–
Other income, with a description of the nature of each item
**7.  Asset Operating Expenses (Opex)**
–
Budget property operating expenses at asset level, consistent with the approved asset business plan and the service charge budget in section 6.
–
Split opex clearly between recoverable (service charge) and non-recoverable (owner) costs.
–
Present the main cost categories separately: property taxes and insurance, utilities, repairs and maintenance, marketing and promotion, property and asset management fees, letting and legal costs, and bad debt provisions.
–
Apply the HQ CPI assumptions for indexation and document any deviation, for example contracted increases or known one-off items.
–
Reconcile the budgeted management fees charged to the asset with the fee income budgeted by the relevant Service Company in Part A.
**8.  Capex**
–
Budget capex at project level, with a short description, the total project cost and the amount phased into 2027 by quarter.
–
Split capex between maintenance capex, development and improvement capex, and leasing capex (tenant incentives and fit-out contributions).
–
State the status of each project — committed, approved or proposed — and the expected impact on income where relevant.
–
Ensure the capex phasing is consistent with the cash reconciliation in section 10.
PART C — ALL ENTITIES
**9.  Corporate Income Tax (Current and Deferred)**
–
Budget the current tax charge on the projected taxable result for each entity, applying the applicable corporate income tax (CIT) rate.

–
Include the utilisation of any available tax losses carried forward, and the resulting cash tax payments by quarter (advance payments and settlements).

**11.3  Specific instructions**
–
**Fee income.** Budget fee income at asset level in the forecasting file. Every line requires the asset code (el2), the fee type (el4) and the detailed description (el5). The el4 + el5 combination defines the fee income type and must be unique. The asset is identified through el2; no counterparty code is required on these lines.
–
**Staff costs.** Budget per employee in the country’s own file, but submit the Coda upload at SCOA level (el4) only. Individual employee detail must not be uploaded.
–
**Genex.** Submit the Coda upload file at SCOA level (el4) with local detail (el5).
–
**Intercompany.** All intercompany income, costs and balances (PP5, PQ12, PR14, PW and PWe) must carry the counterparty entity in el6 and must agree with the central IC Charges workbook. Time charges from and to HQ are submitted at el4 and el6 level.
–
**Asset income.** Budget rental and other income at tenant and unit level in the forecasting file. Submit the Coda upload at SCOA level (el4) with the asset code (el2) and, where the income is attributable to a tenant, the counterparty code (el6). Vacancy, discounts, rent free and turnover rent follow the same tenant-level build-up.
–
**Asset opex and service charges.** Submit at asset level using the asset code (el2), SCOA (el4) and local detail (el5). Service charge expenses require el5 so that the recoverable and non-recoverable split can be traced.
–
**Capex.** Submit per project, using el1 + el2 + el4 + el5 and the Capex ID, so that each project can be linked to the Hub.
–
**Corporate income tax.** Submit current and deferred tax at SCOA level (el4), with the P&L charge and the balance sheet position — tax payable or receivable, deferred tax asset or liability — coded separately.
–
**Cash.** Working capital and cash movements are submitted at el1 + el2 + el4 + el5, at a level that allows the Cash BoP to Cash EoP reconciliation to be explained.
